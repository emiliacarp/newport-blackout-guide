Where to get real information

Radio

107.9 FM

KWVE, "K-Wave". This is the Orange County emergency alert station the city tells residents to tune to. Any battery, crank or car radio will pick it up.

If you have TV power, the city also names Channel 3.

Sirens

Newport Beach has three outdoor sirens. They can sound for any emergency, not just a tsunami. If you hear one, tune to 107.9 FM and wait for instructions.

  • West Jetty View Park, at the Wedge
  • Marina Park, on Balboa Boulevard
  • West Newport Park, near 60th Street and Seashore Drive

They are tested at noon on the first Friday of every month. A siren at that time is only a test.

Do this before anything happens

Reach your neighbors

Door sign

The city's method: every home shows HELP or OK in a front window or on the door. Tap one to turn this phone into the sign, or print the paper version from the Plan tab.

Walking the street? Go first to homes showing HELP and homes showing nothing. Check the OK homes later.

Our street radio plan

Without towers, phones can only reach each other by satellite text, which is slow and outdoors only (see the Satellite tab). A pair of basic walkie-talkies (sold as FRS radios, no license needed) reaches roughly half a mile between houses. They only work if the whole street uses the same channel at the same times.

The channel and times shown in grey are a suggested starting point, not an official city plan. Agree on yours with your neighbors and type them in. They are saved on this phone only.

How to talk on the radio

  • Hold the button, wait one second, then speak. Let go to listen.
  • Say who you are and where: "This is Emilia at number 12."
  • Keep it short. Say "over" when you finish.
  • Someone hurt goes first. Everyone else waits.
  • Turn the radio off between check-in times to save batteries.

Want to do more?

The Fire Department's CERT program trains residents for exactly this: 25 hours, free, age 16 and up. Visit nbcert.org or call 949-644-3112.

Where to go

Tsunami: low ground near the water

West Newport, the Balboa Peninsula, Balboa Island, Lido Isle and the harbor edges are in the city's possible tsunami flood area.

  • After strong shaking, or when told to leave, go inland and uphill. Follow the blue tsunami evacuation signs.
  • Go on foot if traffic is stopped.
  • Stay on high ground. Waves can keep arriving for 8 hours or longer.
  • Go back only when officials say it is safe.

Shelters

Shelter locations are announced when they open. Listen to 107.9 FM. Until you hear one named, home is usually the safest place unless you've been told to leave.

Told to leave? An Immediate Evacuation Order means go now. A Potential Evacuation Order means get ready, and leave early if anyone in your home needs extra time.

Text by satellite

An iPhone 14 or later can send texts through a satellite when there is no cell signal and no Wi-Fi. It needs no tower, no app and no extra equipment. It only switches on when the phone has no signal at all.

Free for two years after the iPhone was first activated, per Apple. Works in the United States with iOS 18 or later.

Emergency: reach 911

  • Go outside, with open sky above you.
  • Call 911. The call may connect even with no bars.
  • If it won't connect, tap Emergency Text via Satellite.
  • Tap Report Emergency and answer the questions by tapping.
  • Point the phone where the screen tells you and hold it there until the message sends.

Text family or a neighbor

  • Go outside with a clear view of the sky. Trees and buildings block it.
  • Open Messages. With no signal, it offers to connect to a satellite. Follow the arrows on screen.
  • Type a short message and send. Expect 30 seconds to a few minutes.
  • Keep the phone pointed at the satellite until it says Delivered.

If Messages doesn't offer it: swipe down from the top right corner, tap the Cellular button, then Satellite.

What it can't do

  • No calls, photos, voice messages or group chats. Plain text to one person at a time.
  • It does not work indoors, in a car or under heavy trees.
  • A neighbor who is also without signal only receives your text when they go outside and connect too. So agree on check-in times.
  • If your phone shows bars but nothing goes through, satellite will not switch on. Use the radio plan.
  • Nobody knows how well it holds up when a whole city uses it at once. Treat it as one tool, not the plan.

Our satellite check-in

Use the same times as the street radio plan on the Neighbors tab. At each time, step outside, connect and send one line.

Say who, where, what you need and when you'll check again. Saved on this phone only.

Practice now, while phones work

Other phones

Older iPhones and most Android phones have no built-in satellite texting. Some newer Android phones do, and some carriers sell a satellite add-on that works on ordinary phones. T-Mobile's is called T-Satellite and is sold to customers of other carriers too.

Plans, prices and supported phones change often. Ask your carrier before you count on it.

For a whole street

  • A satellite internet dish on a battery. A portable dish such as Starlink Mini makes a Wi-Fi spot with real internet. Every phone near it can text and call as usual. It needs a monthly plan and a charged battery or solar panel.
  • Mesh text radios. Small radios (Meshtastic is the common kind, about $30 to $40) pair with a phone and pass texts house to house with no towers. Everyone has to set theirs up before an outage.
  • Walkie-talkies. Still the simplest voice link. See the Neighbors tab.

One home with a dish and a battery can be the street's message point. Put its address in your street radio plan.

Newport Beach Blackout Guide

When the power, Wi-Fi and phones are down. Keep this on the fridge.

Right now

  • Life in danger: call or text 911. iPhone 14 or later with no signal: dial 911 and follow the satellite prompt, outside under open sky.
  • Strong shaking near the water: go inland and uphill. Don't wait for a siren.
  • Smell gas: get out, touch no switches, shut the meter valve with a wrench.
  • Downed power line: stay back, report to 911.
  • Flashlights only. No candles.

First hour

  • Radio to 107.9 FM (a car radio works).
  • Put HELP or OK in a front window.
  • Keep the fridge shut: cold about 4 hours.
  • Unplug electronics. Phone on Low Power Mode. Text, don't call.
  • Check on the neighbors nearest you.

After that

  • Water: 1 gallon per person per day. The water heater tank is drinkable.
  • Unsure water: 8 drops plain unscented bleach per gallon, wait 30 minutes.
  • Dark traffic signal = four-way stop. Stay off the roads.
  • Food above 40°F for 2 hours: throw it out.
  • Generators outside only. Never run a car in the garage.

Information

  • 107.9 FM (KWVE), the county emergency station
  • Sirens mean any emergency: tune to 107.9 FM. Test: first Friday, noon.
  • Emergency: 911 · Information: 311
  • Edison outage line: 1-800-611-1911
  • City: 949-644-3309 · CERT: 949-644-3112
  • Hoag Hospital: 1 Hoag Drive
  • No signal, iPhone 14 or later: go outside, open Messages, follow the satellite prompt. Text only. Check in at our radio times.
